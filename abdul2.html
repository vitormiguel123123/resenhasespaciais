START OF SPACE EXPLORATION

I. END OF THE SECOND WORLD WAR



Germany is Defeated

◆︎ After the end of World War II, the territories that had belonged to Nazi Germany were divided between the United States and its allies and the Soviet Union. The occupation of the country led the two victors to discover various advanced technological weapons created by the Nazis—which had been abandoned following their defeat—including the revolutionary V-2 (*Vergeltungswaffe 2*, or Vengeance Weapon 2), which was powered by a fuel mixture of ethanol and liquid oxygen. Rocket tests conducted by both nations saw the vehicle reach an altitude of 175 km, making it the first human-made object to cross the Kármán line and reach space, though it failed to achieve orbit.





Using blueprints based on advanced Nazi technology, both countries began creating their own V-2-inspired designs, aiming to surpass one another in space technology—a field also influenced by the development of intercontinental missiles, which would prove crucial to the Space Race.
During the 1940s and 1950s, the United States developed the Viking sounding rocket, which provided valuable scientific data from the edge of space between 1949 and 1955, while the Soviets developed the R-1, a tactical ballistic missile based on the design of the German rocket, was never an effective strategic weapon. However, the production and launch of the R-1 provided the Soviets with valuable experience that later enabled the USSR to build its own far more capable rockets.
